Artist Profile: Edward Tait

Edward Tait is a London-based composer, whose music has been performed across the United Kingdom, Ireland and the United States, by ensembles like the London Sinfonietta, Carducci Quartet and Orion Orchestra. He is currently collaborating with the Arquia Duo on recording his song cycle Vectis, based on poems by Mollie Mardel. In November, he will receive his debut broadcast on BBC Radio 3, following success in the NCEM Young Composer Award.

Edward studies at the Royal Academy of Music with Gareth Moorcraft and Louise Drewett, and he is artistic director of the MetroLand Music Festival.

  • Solo Clarinet Sonata (2025)

    Duration: circ. 8 minutes
    Premiere: September 2027 (TBA)

    Click here to purchase the score

    Click here to listen to a recording

    Programme Notes:

    My Solo Clarinet Sonata was one of my most enjoyable pieces to compose, as the instrument allows so much versatility and scope for exploration. This was inspired in part by a shorter solo clarinet piece which I wrote a few months prior, so I decided to take my interest in clarinet writing even further. In particular, I made a conscious effort to start relying a lot more on pure instinct and natural progression, after recent suggestion from my old composition teacher, from a few years back, Brian Elias. Its distinctive structure is one I use quite a lot, opening with a slow introduction, before a drive towards a mini climax, before retreating back to its slow tempo. From there, the piece suddenly jolts into a much quicker tempo, building up slowly and steadily to a substantial climax at the end.

  • Ash Grove (2024)
    for chamber orchestra

    Duration: circ. 8 minutes
    Premiere: Purcell School of Music, 2024

    Click here to purchase the score

    To purchase the parts, please contact the composer directly (email below)

    Click here to listen to a recording

    Programme Notes:

    Ash Grove is probably one of the weirdest ones so far. It is written for Daniel Fardon, director of the Purcell Creative Ensemble, which gave the work its first performance in 2024. Drawing its point of departure from the Welsh traditional tune of the same name, the piece treats this material not as a melody, but as a quiet presence shaping the harmonic and emotional terrain. It is so static, and shouldn’t work in theory, yet somehow, I think it does. It is pastoral in character, yet deliberately static. Through its simplicity, I tried to craft a spiritual purification of harmony and colour, while the chosen instrumentation explores an ecstatic, slightly unreal timbre, suspended between a distant folk memory and something of a dream. I also wanted to take advantage of the space and distance in the CP Hall, with the solo oboist and clarinetist performing their solo parts from the top of the hall, spaced out from everyone else, and from each other.

  • Fleeting Manhattan (2023)
    for orchestra

    Further details for this piece will be added here.

    Click here to listen to / view the available recording or resource

    Programme Notes:

    Fleeting Manhattan was the result of a large chain of brainstorming I did during a trip with my family to New York City. In particular, with Manhattan, there was one thing that stood out, which I couldn’t have told from any video or photo. There is an incredible sense of size distortion which you get when standing in the centre of Central Park, simply by looking up at the skyscrapers. They feel like they are practically hanging over you. Some things can simply only be experienced rather than passed on, but I thought, why not give it a go, in another way, through music. I recently received an orchestral commission, in which to put this to the test, by trying to strike a dynamic contrast between the lively and thrilling streets of Manhattan, and the tranquility of the city's green space. The resulting experience could be one of deep ecstatic fantasy, like an uncanny piece of theatre, but at the same time, reflecting something absolutely beautiful.
